Understanding Cyberbullying

What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is bullying that takes place over digital devices like cell phones, computers, and tablets. It can occur through SMS, apps, online in social media, forums, or gaming.

Common Forms

Posting hurtful comments, spreading rumors online, sharing embarrassing photos/videos, creating fake profiles to harass someone, or excluding others from online groups.

Effects

Victims may experience anxiety, depression, low self-esteem, poor school performance, and in extreme cases, suicidal thoughts.

Educational Video

Key Takeaways:

  • Cyberbullying can happen 24/7 and reach victims even when alone
  • Digital content is permanent and public
  • Bystanders play a crucial role in stopping cyberbullying
  • There are legal consequences for cyberbullying in many jurisdictions

Interactive Activity

Scenario Challenge: What Would You Do?

Scenario 1

You see a classmate posting mean comments about another student on Instagram. The post is getting lots of likes and laughs.

The best responses would be to report the post and/or stand up for the student. Even if you don't comment publicly, you can privately message the victim to offer support.

Scenario 2

Someone creates a fake profile pretending to be you and is sending rude messages to your friends.

You should report the fake profile to the platform and tell a trusted adult. Retaliating by creating another fake profile would only make things worse.
